1. Analysis of Wikipedia's robots.txt

- Which sections of the website are restricted for crawling?
Most of the website is open for crawling, but there are many specific User-agents that are completely blocked (Disallow: /). 
Examples of restricted bots include: MJ12bot, Mediapartners-Google, UbiCrawler, Zao, sitecheck.internetseer.com, Zealbot, 
MSIECrawler, HTTrack, wget, grub-client, and many others. 
These are mostly bad bots, site copiers, or very aggressive crawlers.

- Are there specific rules for certain user agents?
Yes. Wikipedia has many specific rules for different User-agents. 
Some bots (like IsraBot and Orthogaffe) are allowed full access (Disallow: is empty). 
However, many bots are completely blocked with "Disallow: /". 
The default rule for general crawlers (User-agent: *) is not heavily restricted.

2. Reflection on robots.txt

Websites use robots.txt to communicate with web crawlers and scrapers about which parts of their site can or cannot be accessed. 
It serves as a voluntary ethical guideline that helps prevent server overload, protects private or sensitive areas, and stops 
malicious bots from copying entire websites. 

By respecting robots.txt, scrapers demonstrate responsibility and ethical behavior. This promotes a healthier internet ecosystem 
where websites are more willing to share public data, knowing that responsible developers will not abuse their resources.
